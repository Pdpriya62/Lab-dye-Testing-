IoT Day · RV University

A Low-Cost Multi-Sensor IoT Rig for
Process-Aware Lab-Dip Shade Assessment

Team 11 · School of Computer Science and Engineering, RV University, Bengaluru

1Aim

To build a low-cost IoT rig that records every colour reading of a textile lab dip together with the dye-bath and room conditions that produced it, tagged with the sample ID, so that a failed dip can be traced to a likely cause instead of guessed at.

2Problem Statement

  • A lab dip is a small swatch dyed to the buyer's shade before bulk dyeing.
  • When a dip is rejected, the recipe is blamed, yet bath temperature, lighting and humidity also shift the shade.
  • Labs rarely record these beside the colour reading, so a rejected dip has no cause attached. Each extra round wastes dye, water and a day.
  • Spectrophotometers are costly and do not log the process.

4Working Principle

Sensecolour, bath, room, light
ESP32Lab, ΔE, bath alert
MQTTJSON every 3 s
↓ subscribed by ↓
AppΔE, PASS / FAIL, commands
Laptoplog, compare, analyse
ESP32-CAMphoto → Lab, grid
  1. Colour: TCS34725 counts ÷ clear channel, scaled to 0–255, corrected by calibration gains, then sRGB → XYZ (D65) → CIE L*a*b*.
  2. Target: the reference swatch is captured from the app. Every later swatch gets ΔE and PASS / FAIL against it.
  3. Process: bath temperature (alert beyond ±2 °C of set point), room temperature, humidity and light go in the same record.
  4. Camera: each photo is corrected pixel by pixel with a white-paper photo, the fabric patch is found automatically and a 3 × 3 grid checks dyeing levelness. Results show on the app's camera card.
  5. Analysis: correlation, OLS with ANOVA and cross-validated regression rank the most likely cause of a colour deviation.
  6. Safety: every command is range-checked. A missing sensor sends nothing, never a fake value.

5Measurement Box & Calibration

  • The camera's own flash bounces off the white ceiling: soft light, no glare, no extra LEDs.
  • The TCS sits in its own opaque box a few mm above the fabric.
  • A foam rim seals out room light. The room sensors stay outside on the breadboard.
Calibrated on plain white paper
TCS34725gain 1×, 154 ms. Per-channel gains R 1.0, G 1.585, B 2.708 turn white paper from R130 G82 B48 into neutral grey.
ESP32-CAMwhite balance and exposure locked (exposure 150, no clipping). A white-paper photo corrects every later photo pixel by pixel.
Re-checktake a new white photo whenever the box, camera or lighting changes.

6Live Demo

A glass of water with ink stands in for the dye bath; dyed fabric swatches go under the box.

  1. Reference fabric under the box, tap Capture in the app: ΔE ≈ 0.
  2. Swap in another shade: ΔE rises, PASS / FAIL updates on OLED and phone.
  3. Camera photo: Lab value and levelness grid appear on the app.
  4. Warm the probe by hand out of the ink: bath alert beyond ±2 °C.

7Results

Real rig, white-paper calibration

White paper on the TCS34725 now reads neutral (a −0.14, b 0.22). The camera's green cast is removed by the white reference: a yellow swatch reads L 78.4, a −9.7, b 48.3. Both sensors agree on its hue: 98° vs 101°.
